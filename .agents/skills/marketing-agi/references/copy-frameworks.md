# Frameworks

Read this before generating. Generating from memory collapses toward the same three angles every time; this file forces spread.

---

## Awareness stages

The most important single decision, and the one most often skipped. Copy pitched at the wrong stage fails no matter how well written it is.

| Stage | Reader state | What the copy must lead with |
|---|---|---|
| **Unaware** | Doesn't know they have the problem | The problem, dramatised. No product. |
| **Problem-aware** | Feels the pain, doesn't know solutions exist | That a category of solution exists |
| **Solution-aware** | Knows the category, not your product | Why this mechanism beats the alternatives |
| **Product-aware** | Knows you, hasn't bought | Objection handling, proof, risk reversal |
| **Most-aware** | Ready, waiting for a reason | The offer. Price, terms, deadline. |

Traffic source is the tell. Cold paid social skews unaware/problem-aware. Branded search and retargeting skew product-aware/most-aware. Writing unaware copy for a retargeting audience wastes the most expensive impressions you buy.

## Angle taxonomy

Force at least eight distinct angles in generation. These are genuinely different arguments, not rephrasings.

1. **Problem-agitation** — name the pain, make it present-tense and specific
2. **Specific outcome** — the number, the timeframe, the before/after
3. **Contrarian** — challenge a belief the audience holds
4. **Identity** — "for people who [self-image]"
5. **Mechanism** — *why* it works; the named process
6. **Direct offer** — what you get, what it costs, what happens next
7. **Objection-first** — lead with the reason not to buy, then dismantle it
8. **Comparison** — against the named alternative or the status quo
9. **Social proof** — the named customer, the specific result
10. **Cost of inaction** — what the status quo costs per month
11. **Curiosity gap** — use sparingly; never write a cheque the page can't cash
12. **Insider knowledge** — the thing practitioners know that buyers don't

## Classical structures

**PAS** — Problem, Agitate, Solution. Fast, works cold, works short.

**AIDA** — Attention, Interest, Desire, Action. General-purpose long-form.

**BAB** — Before, After, Bridge. Strong when transformation is visual or measurable.

**4Ps** — Picture, Promise, Prove, Push. Landing pages.

**RMBC** — Research, Mechanism, Brief, Copy. A process rather than a template: research the market and the product, isolate the *unique mechanism* that explains why it works, brief the argument, then write. The mechanism step is the valuable one and the one usually skipped — it is what makes a claim non-copyable by a competitor.

**Rule of One** — one reader, one big idea, one promise, one action. Use as a check on any draft: if you can't name each of the four, the copy is unfocused.

## Headline formula groups

When generating headlines, force at least one from each group — the groups fail differently, which is the point of testing across them:

| Group | The move | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| **Outcome** | Name the after-state with a number or timeframe | "[Result] in [timeframe], without [pain]" |
| **Problem** | Name the pain more precisely than the reader can | "Still [doing the painful workaround]?" |
| **Audience** | Call the reader out by role or situation | "For [specific person] who [specific situation]" |
| **Differentiation** | Claim the thing only this product can say | "The only [category] that [unique mechanism]" |
| **Proof** | Lead with the evidence itself | "[Named customer] [specific result]. Here's the setup." |

A headline set drawn from one group is a tone test, not a message test.

## Offer construction

The offer usually matters more than the copy. When a piece is failing, check the offer before rewriting words.

Strengthen along five axes:

- **Dream outcome** — what they actually want, stated concretely
- **Perceived likelihood of achievement** — proof, guarantees, track record, specificity
- **Time delay** — how fast they see the first result
- **Effort and sacrifice** — what they have to do, and what they have to give up

Raise the first two, lower the last two — as an equation: **perceived value = (dream outcome × likelihood) ÷ (time delay × effort)**. Most weak offers fail on *perceived likelihood* — the promise is fine, nobody believes it.

**Risk reversal** is the cheapest lever available: guarantee, free trial, no card required, pay-after-results. If a client won't offer any, ask why — the answer is usually that they don't believe their own retention numbers, which is the real problem.

**Urgency must be real.** Fake countdown timers and invented scarcity work once and cost the brand permanently. If there is no genuine deadline, use cost-of-delay instead.

## Channel constraints

| Channel | Hard limits | What actually matters |
|---|---|---|
| Email subject | ~40 chars visible mobile | Specificity beats cleverness; curiosity decays fast across sends |
| Meta primary text | ~125 chars before truncation | First line carries everything |
| Google RSA headline | 30 chars | Include the query term; write 15, let the system mix |
| Google RSA description | 90 chars | One benefit, one proof point |
| Landing hero | ~10 words | Who it's for + what changes |
| LinkedIn post | ~200 chars before "see more" | The break must land mid-tension |
| Short-form video hook | 1.5-3 seconds | Visual and verbal hook must fire simultaneously |
| App Store title | 30 chars | Brand + primary term |
| App Store subtitle | 30 chars | Second term + outcome |

Write to the truncation point, not the character limit. What survives the fold is the whole ad.

## Testing discipline

Test **one variable at a time** or you learn nothing. Two headlines that differ in angle, length and tone produce an uninterpretable result.

Test in this order — the earlier items have far higher variance:
1. Offer
2. Angle
3. Headline
4. Body
5. CTA wording
6. Everything else

Most CTA-button-colour tests are a way of avoiding the offer conversation.

Do not declare a winner on a handful of conversions. If the sample is small, say the result is directional and name what sample size would settle it.
