# Launch

A launch is not an announcement. It is a manufactured spike of attention pointed at one conversion action. Most launches fail before launch day, because the list, the assets and the story were supposed to exist already.

Read `brand-context.md`. Establish before planning:
1. **What ships** — product, feature, pricing change, content asset. Feature launches are smaller than founders think; price launches are bigger.
2. **The one metric** — signups, revenue, installs, waitlist, PH rank. A launch pointed at two metrics hits neither.
3. **What exists** — email list size, social following, community, press contacts, partner network. The launch can only spend distribution that already exists; launch week is the wrong time to build it.
4. **The date, and whether it's movable.** Immovable dates cut scope, not quality.

## The launch equation

Launch outcome ≈ (audience you can reach) × (story worth repeating) × (friction to act). Work the weakest factor first. Most teams polish assets (factor 3) while the real constraint is factor 1 — nobody to tell.

If the reachable audience is near zero, say so plainly and restructure: the "launch" becomes the start of a distribution-building arc, not a day.

## Phases

### T-4 to T-2 weeks — the seeding phase
- Lock the story: one sentence, one enemy (the old way), one number. Route through `positioning.md` if the positioning isn't settled — launching an unpositioned product wastes the spike.
- Build the asset stack (below) and get every claim verified now, not launch morning
- Warm the channels: 2-3 problem-space posts (no product) via `social.md`; email 1-2 of the launch sequence via `email.md`
- Recruit day-one voices: 10-30 people who will genuinely comment/share/review within the first hours. Ask individually. This list predicts launch day better than any asset.
- Line up the "where else": newsletters, communities, podcasts, partner posts scheduled for launch week

### T-1 week — the freeze
- Feature freeze; launch week absorbs zero new scope
- Dry-run the conversion path end to end on mobile — the single most common launch-day failure is a broken or slow signup flow discovered at peak traffic
- Pre-write everything: the posts, the emails, the replies to the 10 predictable questions, the response to the predictable criticism
- Confirm the day-one voices with the exact time and the exact ask

### Launch day — sequencing
1. Ship the primary post/listing at the platform's optimal time
2. Personal-note wave to the day-one list within minutes — individual, not BCC
3. Email blast to the list (launch-sequence email 3, the open)
4. Founder posts on personal accounts — personal outperforms brand on every platform
5. Reply to *everything* for the first 3-4 hours; early engagement compounds distribution on every surface
6. Community drops where genuinely welcome — participate, don't spam; a community post from a stranger is noise, from a member it's news
7. Evening: results-so-far post. Momentum reporting is content.

### T+1 week — the harvest
Most teams stop at day 1 and forfeit half the value:
- Day 2: the "how we built it" or "what we learned" post
- Day 3-4: objection-teardown and case-study emails (sequence 5-6)
- Day 5-7: the numbers retrospective — launch metrics shared openly are themselves a high-performing post
- Collect every question asked during launch; they are the FAQ, the next content calendar, and the objection list for `copy.md`

## Product Hunt specifics

Verify current PH mechanics before the run — the platform changes rules and the community's norms drift. Durable truths:

- Launch at 00:01 Pacific for the full 24-hour voting window
- The hunter matters less than it used to; the maker's own network matters more
- First-hour velocity heavily shapes the day — the day-one voice list *is* the PH strategy
- Tagline ≤ 60 chars: outcome, not category. Gallery first image = the value prop, not the logo. Maker comment = the story and the honest "why", not the feature list.
- **Never buy or trade upvotes, never run upvote-exchange pods** — detection unranks the launch, and the traffic quality from rank-gaming is worthless anyway
- PH traffic is browsers, not buyers: expect signups and feedback, not revenue. Set the internal expectation before the day, or the team reads a good launch as a failure.

## The asset stack

Everything, pre-written and verified before T-1:

| Asset | Route through |
|---|---|
| One-sentence story + 3 proof points | `positioning.md` |
| Launch email sequence (8) | `email.md` |
| Founder posts, LinkedIn + X, day 0 through day 7 | `social.md` |

| FAQ / objection replies (the 10 predictable ones) | `copy.md` |

## Honesty

Do not project launch numbers — the variance is enormous and the projection will be wrong in a direction that damages trust. Give the levers and the leading indicators (list size × historical CTR gives a floor for day-one traffic; that's as far as honest math goes). If the plan depends on an audience that doesn't exist yet, the deliverable is a distribution plan with a launch at the end of it, and the report should say exactly that.
